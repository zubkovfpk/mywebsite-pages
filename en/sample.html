“Institute N”: drafting design documentation from historical analogues

Energy-sector design institute · about 900 employees · prepared by the bureau at the client’s request

Synthetic example: the organisation, figures and findings are fictional; any resemblance is coincidental. The structure follows the bureau’s real assessments.

1Key findings

  • The request is realistic, but AI fits only part of it. The institute already produces specifications and drawings from its 3D model and equipment database – that is conventional automation. AI is needed to find analogues in the unstructured archive and to draft text sections.
  • Three scenarios with the greatest effect: hybrid analogue search; drafts of text sections with a link to every source fragment; drafts of replies to review comments.
  • A partner, not a boxed product. The institute has a strong in-house IT team. The proposal should strengthen it: method, closed-environment architecture, quality evaluation, AI security.
  • The main risks are legal and regulatory, not technological: clients’ rights to documentation, security-sensitive data on energy facilities, possible critical-infrastructure status.
  • Urgent measure: stop uploading design documentation to public cloud chatbots until the closed environment is running.
  • Next step: 6–8 weeks of extended diagnostics on 3–5 completed projects of one type, then a 3–4-month pilot on 1–2 text sections. Order of effect: 10–33 full-time equivalents (hypothesis).

2Scope and inputs

The baseline diagnostics used the request, open sources and four client documents, without access to internal systems. All figures are hypotheses to be verified in extended diagnostics. The analysis was prepared by the lead agent and the seven expert roles of Council 7+1; legal, financial and industry findings were reviewed by human experts. Legal, compliance and security vetoes are binding.

5Key trade-offs

Specifications. Item, code and quantity accuracy must be absolute, and a source of truth already exists. A generative model lowers quality here: a non-zero share of wrong items an engineer may miss.

RAG or fine-tuning. Fine-tuning bakes outdated solutions and different clients’ data into the model and gives no source links. RAG preserves provenance, rights and currency.

6Urgent measures

  • An interim AI-use policy: no uploads of design documentation to external services.
  • A list of sections excluded from the corpus on security grounds.

7Target process

  1. The engineer sets the facility type and section → rules select the permitted corpus by access rights.
  2. AI finds analogues and drafts text with a link to every fragment.
  3. Automatic checks reconcile numbers and standards references with sources.
  4. The engineer reviews, edits and signs; the log records the source of every fragment.

10Effect model

FTE = N × d × k: N – designers working on text sections (≈520); d – share of their time spent on analogue search, texts and replies to comments (8–14 %); k – reduction of that time (25–45 %). Result: 10–33 FTE. All parameters are assumptions to be measured.

11Scope of extended diagnostics

  • Data and corpus: archive inventory, trial recognition.
  • Processes: interviews with chief project engineers, time measurement.
  • Legal and compliance: contract audit, critical-infrastructure status.
  • Architecture and security: threat model, test of 2–3 local models.
  • Economics: effect model, TCO, continue / stop criteria.

12Questions for the client

  • Which design sections are the most labour-intensive and repetitive?
  • How large is the archive and what share is scanned?
  • Are there categorised critical-infrastructure objects?
  • How do contracts govern reuse of documentation?
  • Are GPU resources available in the environment?
  • Who owns the process and leads the pilot?

13Dissenting views

  • Industry expert: some features may come from the CAD vendor – focus the pilot on archive knowledge.
  • Financial expert: if measurement shows d < 8 %, start with option A.
  • AI security: no pilot on the real corpus without fragment-level access control.
