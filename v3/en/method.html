Method · decision before deployment

First decide whether AI is needed. Then work.

We do not sell implementation. We issue a comprehensive expert assessment of every process – technological, economic, legal and industry-specific. And the bureau has the right to show where AI is not needed.

Four verdicts

A verdict for every process

AI is justified

Unstructured data, variable patterns, scale, predictive value.

Oil-slick detection from drone imagery: 91% accuracy where the sea area used to be patrolled by vessel.

Hybrid

AI for some subtasks; rules and people for critical decisions.

A developer's document flow: LLM in 3 of 8 subtasks, only where accuracy exceeds 95%.

No AI

Stable rules, low volume, zero tolerance for error, not enough data.

Warning a vessel approaching an ice zone is deterministic logic: route geometry and time thresholds.

Defer

A sound idea, but too early: little data, processes not ready, or no owner.

First put data and procedures in order; return to AI when there is something to learn from and someone accountable.

Scoring

A 0–4 scale and a radar: current profile against the required one

  1. 0Absent or unsuitable – and demonstrably so
  2. 1Ad hoc, substantial gaps
  3. 2Partially established, material gaps
  4. 3Adequate for the stated deployment stage
  5. 4Established, tested, monitored
  • “Not assessed” is not zero – an evidence gap shows as a break in the radar line, not a dip to the centre
  • No average score – a weak factor cannot be offset by a strong one; we do not compute radar area
  • Targets depend on the stage – a human-supervised pilot and production use need different profiles
  • Every score has evidence – sources, assumptions, confidence and evidence coverage – kept separate from the score
  • A working rubric – the scale is calibrated on reference cases and versioned

Decision

Recommendation and deployment status are two separate decisions

Recommendation – what to use

  • AIa model where rules cannot describe the task
  • HybridAI for some subtasks; rules and people for critical decisions
  • No AIdeterministic automation: rules, integrations, reporting
  • Deferdata, procedures or a process owner come first

Deployment status – can it go ahead now

  • Pilot eligiblesufficient evidence, no binding block, all prerequisites met – a human-supervised pilot can start
  • Eligible after prerequisitesclose the prerequisites in the roadmap first
  • Blockeda verified legal, compliance or security block
  • Insufficient evidencecritical evidence gaps – more information is needed

Order of checks. A legal, compliance or security block means “blocked” regardless of scores. Otherwise, critical gaps mean “insufficient evidence”. Otherwise, unmet prerequisites mean “eligible after prerequisites”.

An “AI” recommendation is not permission to deploy. Pilot eligibility is not production approval: production needs its own required profile and sign-off.

What the assessment contains

Baseline diagnostics

From open data and your materials, without access to internal systems

  • Key findings – the decision on one page and urgent measures
  • Scope and inputs – what the findings rest on; all figures are hypotheses
  • Profile and maturity – what you already have, so nothing is rebuilt
  • Information sufficiency – for every process: sufficient, partial, insufficient
  • Decision matrix – recommendation, conditions and rationale for every subtask
  • Key trade-offs – why the contested choices were made
  • Target process – where AI works, where rules do, where a person decides
  • Risk register – security, legal and compliance: basis, consequence, mitigation
  • Roadmap options – A/B/C and when to choose each
  • Effect model – formula and assumptions as a range
  • Questions and dissent – what to clarify and where experts disagreed

Extended diagnostics

With access to data and people. Everything in the baseline, plus:

  • Readiness radar – eight factors: current and required profile; for broad audits, a heatmap
  • Evidence table – score, sources, confidence and coverage for every factor
  • Deployment status – for every process: pilot, prerequisites, blocked or insufficient evidence
  • Environment architecture – data categorisation and infrastructure options with 3-year TCO
  • Business case – on a measured baseline
  • Implementation phases – transition criteria and continue / stop points
  • Signature – the chief designer and the experts who reviewed the findings

Accountability

What the bureau is accountable for

  • Assessment – the soundness of every verdict: technology, economics, law, industry
  • Architecture – the target solution design and the limits of AI autonomy
  • Numbers – the method and assumptions behind every effect estimate
  • Design supervision – conformity of the implementation with the assessment – whether by our team or by external developers and contractors
  • Governance – monitoring, KRIs and a human in the loop on critical decisions

FAQ

The method in brief

What if AI turns out to be unnecessary?

That is not a refusal but a saving: you get a decision matrix and a conventional-automation roadmap – and spend the budget on what works. For the bureau it is as successful a project as an implementation.

Do you work with closed environments?

Yes: on-prem MLOps, sovereign LLM stacks, personal data law compliance, isolated environments without external SaaS. Our key implementations are exactly there.

Can we implement in-house or with another contractor?

Yes. Then the bureau provides design supervision: it checks that the solution matches the assessment and architecture, accepts phases and tracks metrics.

How are you different from a systems integrator?

An integrator earns on implementation, so it is interested in implementing. The bureau earns on the assessment and is accountable for it, so it can honestly say where AI is not needed.
